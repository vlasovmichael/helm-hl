What to take away
  • A market order eats the book from the top down: best price first, then worse. The average fill is always worse than what you saw before clicking — that is slippage.
  • The bigger the size, the deeper you eat and the worse the average. Double the size and slippage more than doubles.
  • A limit order eats nothing — it adds size to the book. A large limit order is a “level”: price stalls against it because someone has to trade through it first.
  • A stop-market is a market order at the worst possible moment. That is where the gap between “a 1.5% stop” and the real loss comes from.
